# 06 — PDF assembly on Chromium-printed parts (measurement)

The earlier assembly experiment (`spikes/03-pdf-assembly/`) merged and
numbered partial PDFs that PDFsharp had generated. The product's partial PDFs
will be printed by Chromium. This experiment repeats the same five assembly
variants, the same measurements and the same independent checks on partial
PDFs **printed by Chromium** through the layout harness of
`spikes/02-pdf-layout/`, and reports what changes.

It has two parts:

1. a **producer** (`PdfLayoutSpike/`): the layout harness's builder, runner,
   accounting checks and Paged.js 0.4.3, copied byte for byte from commit
   `eebc9ba`, driven by a new adapter (`PartSetProducer.cs`, `Program.cs`)
   that prints sets of 50 / 250 / 500 parts of 10–30 pages (1 000 / 5 000 /
   10 000 pages) with exactly the per-part page counts of the earlier
   experiment;
2. the **assembly harness** (`PdfAssemblySpike/`) of the earlier experiment,
   copied from commit `f88b9f0`, with only the changes the new input forces
   (see *What changed in the harness and why*).

`eebc9ba` and `f88b9f0` are the commit identifiers after the repository
history was rewritten; before the rewrite they were `6c809ca` and `115e031`.

The code is disposable; the measurements below are kept. **No decision is
taken here.** This run follows the third revision of this experiment's
measurement plan.

## Hypothesis

- **H1** — Chromium-printed parts pass the structural check clean, and merged
  or stamped outputs keep passing it.
- **H2** — memory and time per variant are of the same order as on the
  generated parts of the earlier experiment.
- **H3** — stamping changes nothing outside the numbering area.
- **H4** — the parts' own margin content leaves no room for the stamp in the
  earlier experiment's bottom band.

"Bounded" keeps the earlier experiment's convention, fixed before measuring
and not a target: a variant is **bounded** when its median peak RSS at
10 000 pages is at most 1.5 × its median peak RSS at 1 000 pages (for the
stamp-then-qpdf variant, the larger of its two steps). A failed or killed
10 000-page run is "not bounded".

## Input rows

The parts are cut from the **ordinary records** of the dataset: a record is
ordinary exactly when its description has fewer than 1 500 characters (UTF-16
code units). The **1 990** records with descriptions of 1 500 characters or
more — 1 % of the dataset, the generator's own long-description class, the
same count its `check` reports as "long (>=1500)" — are left out of every
part. In this dataset no description falls between 610 and 1 499 characters
(the longest kept is 609, the shortest left out is 1 500), so any limit from
610 to 1 500 selects the same records. The limit was fixed before any part was
laid out; it was not derived from layout results.

Inside each set's range of used sequences, the records left out number **165**
(1 000 pages), **821** (5 000) and **1 659** (10 000). Every set manifest records
the rule, the limit, the excluded count and the boundary values, and, per
part, the first and last sequence, the ordinary records used and the
sequences left out inside its span. Before measuring, the experiment checked
every set against the dataset: every used description is below 1 500
characters (the longest used is 606 / 608 / 609), the used sequences strictly
increase, the gaps between them are exactly the records left out and nothing
else, the first part starts at the first ordinary record, each part's record
count equals the ordinary records inside its span, and the excluded count is
1 990 — all four sets passed.

What this means for the results:

- The earlier layout experiment's ordinary blocks **included such rows** (up
  to about 2 500 characters, able to span pages). These parts therefore hold
  **no page-spanning row** and **do not reproduce the real row mix**. The
  product's partial PDFs will contain long rows; **that is not measured
  here**.
- The reason for leaving them out: with long rows kept, some exact page
  counts of the earlier experiment's lists cannot be reached by any
  contiguous record count (an attempt stopped at the fifth part of the
  1 000-page set: 120 records gave fewer than 11 pages and 121 gave more; the
  121st record has a 2 395-character description). The cause is inferred
  from the data; the layouts of that attempt were not recorded.
- The effect is limited to content-dependent figures (bytes and text per
  page). Page counts, page order, numbering and the pixel checks are
  unaffected, because the page-count lists are the earlier experiment's and
  every check judges each page against its own source page.

No decision and no recommendation follow from this choice.

## Where the stamp lands

Every part carries its own margin content, written by the layout harness:
the report title at the top left, the part's **own "Page k of n"** at the top
right (numbered within the part), and a footer with "Brought forward",
"Page subtotal" and "Carried forward" at the bottom left, centre and right.

- **The earlier experiment's numbering band is occupied.** It stamped
  "Page X of Y" centred in the bottom 15 mm of the page. Here that band holds
  words on **every source page** — 1 000 of 1 000, 5 000 of 5 000 and
  10 000 of 10 000 pages (and 1 000 of 1 000 in the second production) — always
  the same pattern, `Brought forward: # Page subtotal: # Carried forward: #`
  (digits shown as `#`). A stamp there would sit on the page subtotal.
- **The numbering area used here** is the top centre, the one margin area
  empty on every page of these parts: 70–140 mm across, 4–14 mm from the top
  (198.43–396.85 pt by 11.34–39.69 pt; 197–398 × 10–41 px at 72 dpi), with the
  stamp's baseline 10 mm below the top edge, in the same font (DejaVu Sans
  8 pt), colour and drawing call as before. Before measuring, no word of any
  source page lay in it, and every pixel of it was white on every rendered
  reference page (all 1 000 pages at 1 000 pages, 30 sampled pages at 5 000 and
  10 000, on both references).
- **A merged page shows two numbers**: the part's own "Page k of n" at the top
  right and the global "Page i of N" at the top centre. The checks confirm the
  part's own number travels unchanged.

The empty top centre and the block-local counter are properties of this
measurement fixture (the layout harness's header), not product facts. Where
the product places its global page number is not decided here.

## How to run

From the repository root, PowerShell. The engine of this machine runs in WSL,
so bind-mount sources take the `/mnt/<drive>/…` form.

```powershell
$spike  = "spikes/06-pdf-assembly-chromium-parts"
$wslRoot = "/mnt/" + $PWD.Drive.Name.ToLowerInvariant() + ($PWD.Path.Substring(2) -replace '\\', '/')

# 1. Dataset, into the spike's ignored work folder; it must hash to dataset.sha256.
dotnet run --project samples/datasets/ReportService.Samples.DatasetGenerator -c Release -- `
  generate --dataset grouped --count 200000 --output "$spike/work/datasets"

# 2. Images. The PDF base digest comes from the local registry; qpdf from its release.
docker buildx build --progress=plain --load `
  --build-arg "PDF_BASE_DIGEST=sha256:20a7c39a219fe9ad039a8cda5d31cbc0c3e1d1166b729cc1c719b4ccd09de60d" `
  --build-arg PRODUCER_CODE_COMMIT=eebc9ba `
  -f "$spike/Dockerfile.producer" -t pdf-assembly-chromium-producer:local .
docker build --progress=plain -f "$spike/Dockerfile" `
  --build-arg QPDF_VERSION=12.4.2 `
  --build-arg QPDF_SHA256=db367d897829f22c4198ce1094143c9d467bd6ee7dfabc44ba6f02056b24f8b1 `
  -t pdf-assembly-chromium:local .

# 3. Parts: 1000, 5000, 10000 (one container run each), then 1000 again as 1000-repeat.
$ts = Get-Date -Format yyMMddHHmmss
docker run --rm --init --network none --shm-size=1g --name "pdf-assembly-chromium-producer-$ts" `
  -v "$wslRoot/$spike/work/datasets:/data:ro" -v "$wslRoot/$spike/dataset.sha256:/sums/dataset.sha256:ro" `
  -v "$wslRoot/spikes/03-pdf-assembly/results/verification.json:/baseline/verification.json:ro" `
  -v "$wslRoot/$spike/work/parts:/out" pdf-assembly-chromium-producer:local `
  produce --pages 1000 --dataset /data/header-items-v1.grouped.200k.json --sums /sums/dataset.sha256 `
  --baseline /baseline/verification.json --out /out
#    the same with --pages 5000, --pages 10000, and (at least a minute later) --pages 1000 --set-name 1000-repeat

# 4. Measure (no other measuring container running).
New-Item -ItemType Directory -Force "$spike/results/raw" | Out-Null
$ts = Get-Date -Format yyMMddHHmmss
$hostContainers = (docker ps --format "{{.Names}} {{.Image}} {{.Status}}") -join "; "
docker run --rm --init --network none --name "pdf-assembly-chromium-$ts" `
  --memory 8g --memory-swap 8g --cpus 4 `
  -e "HOST_CONTAINERS_BEFORE=$hostContainers" `
  -v "$wslRoot/$spike/work/parts:/parts:ro" -v "$wslRoot/$spike/work/datasets:/data:ro" `
  -v "$wslRoot/$spike/results/raw:/out" `
  pdf-assembly-chromium:local matrix --parts /parts --dataset /data/header-items-v1.grouped.200k.json `
  --out /out --work /work --repetitions 3
```

- `produce --cross-check-only` stops after comparing the set's page-count list
  with the earlier experiment's (exit 0 equal, 1 different). A part that
  cannot reach its page count stops production (exit 1) and writes every
  layout tried to `production-failure.json` in the set folder.
- `produce-probe` prints a 2-part set of 10 pages each; `probe --parts DIR
  --out DIR` in the measuring image runs the library checks on it
  (*Library behaviour checked before measuring*).
- `matrix … --quick`: 1 000 pages, one repetition, full verification.
- The checks on their own, in the measuring image: `input-rows`, `controls`,
  `repro`, `verify` (the usage is printed when no command is given).
- From Git Bash, add `MSYS_NO_PATHCONV=1`; Windows-path mounts (`D:\…`) fail
  on this machine.

Before measuring, `matrix` copies the sets into the container, checks every
part's hash, the input rows against the dataset, the fixture (page boxes and
the rounded references, numbering area empty, pages unique, own counter,
bottom band), the two productions of the 1 000-page set, the 29 controls and
every source part; any unexpected result writes `summary.json` and
`verification.json` with `stoppedBeforeMeasuring` and exits 1 with nothing
measured. Exit codes: 0 every check passed, 1 a check failed or a run errored,
2 invalid arguments. Memory, time and disk never decide the exit code.

Outputs: `results/raw/` (ignored) receives `runs.csv`, `summary.json`,
`verification.json` and `production.json`; the copies in `results/` are the
ones published here (`production.json` with a note on the parallel
production). `results/samples/` holds the first part of the 1 000-page set
(`partial-part-0001.pdf`, 12 pages) and its first three parts merged and
numbered by V2 (`three-parts-numbered.pdf`, 49 pages; `verify --pages all`
PASS).

### PDFsharp 6.2.4 import rewrites the page box of Chromium parts

A finding about third-party behaviour, not about the product.

- Chromium writes every page box as `/MediaBox [0 0 594.95996 841.91998]`.
  Every page PDFsharp 6.2.4 writes after importing it (V1, V2, V3, V5) carries
  `[0 0 594.96 841.92]` — on all 1 000 / 5 000 / 10 000 pages of every output.
  qpdf (V4) keeps the box exactly as written.
- The change is about 4e-5 pt (about 7e-8 relative), yet at 72 dpi poppler
  renders it differently: against the **unedited** source page, V1 changes
  1 712–14 982 pixels per page outside the numbering area at 1 000 pages
  (6 952 984 in total over 1 000 pages), 1 938–11 625 on the 30 sampled pages
  at 5 000 and 3 979–11 969 at 10 000. This is a **rasterisation effect, not a
  content change**: the words of every page are unchanged.
- **The effect comes from the height number alone.** On the first part of
  the 1 000-page set, changing only `594.95996` → `594.96000` (same length,
  bytes edited in place) changes **0** pixels on pages 1–3; changing only
  `841.91998` → `841.92000` changes **11 609, 7 660 and 5 374**, exactly as
  many as changing both. (On the earlier 2-part probe set the same edit
  changed 11 610, 7 661 and 5 375.) Rendering flips the page top-down, so a
  2e-5 pt difference in the height becomes a vertical offset that moves
  pixels; the width does not.
- **What changed from the original rule.** The pixel rule stays strict: 0
  changed pixels outside the numbering area, 0 anywhere on merge-only outputs.
  The only thing tolerated is the page-box text, and only on pages PDFsharp
  wrote: such a page may carry the source box or the source box rounded to
  two decimals, and it is compared with a reference built for that exact box.
  The rounded reference of each part is made from a copy of the part by a
  same-length, in-place substitution of the box numbers only (`594.95996` →
  `594.96000`, `841.91998` → `841.92000`) — no PDF library or rewrite touches
  it — and is checked to differ in exactly 8 bytes per page, to keep its size
  and its structural-check result; it lives only in the container's temporary
  folder and is deleted after its hash is recorded. V4 must keep the source
  box. Any other box value fails the output.
- **Its controls** (base: the first part of the 1 000-page set, 12 pages): BC-1
  building the rounded reference passes every check (96 differing bytes, all
  inside the box numbers); BC-2 the part under the qpdf rule against the
  unedited reference — 0 pixels on every page; BC-3 the rounded copy under the
  PDFsharp rule against the rounded reference — 0 pixels on every page (86 020
  against the unedited reference, the size of the finding); BC-4 a box of
  `[0 0 594.97000 841.92000]` — rejected under both rules (its pixels: 86 020
  against the unedited reference and 0 against the rounded one, because it
  changes the width only, so it matches the rounded reference by
  construction — that is why the box text is checked and not only pixels);
  BC-5 the rounded copy under the qpdf rule — rejected; BC-6 the rounded copy
  with page 1's content shifted by 0.5 pt — fails on 72 261 pixels outside the
  numbering area, so the rounded reference cannot hide a real difference;
  BC-7 the same with a 0.1 pt shift — 26 141 pixels, recorded: **detected at
  0.1 pt; smaller shifts not tested**. The BC-6 and BC-7 inputs were made by
  editing an uncompressed (`--qdf`) form of the part with the stream length
  adjusted, then rewriting it with plain `qpdf`; that rewrite exits 3 with
  "file is damaged", "(offset 247287): xref not found", "Attempting to
  reconstruct cross-reference table" and "operation succeeded with warnings;
  resulting file may have some problems"; the results pass `qpdf --check`
  clean and render all 12 pages.
- No library is chosen and nothing is recommended because of this.

### Reproducibility

- **Within the run:** after the copy into the container and again after the
  last measured step, every one of the 850 parts had its manifest SHA-256 —
  every repetition read the same bytes.
- **Across two productions** of the 1 000-page set (separate containers, the
  second 62 s after the first ended): **50 of 50 parts content-identical** —
  same structure (order, file, pages, first global page, first and last
  sequence, records), same words on every page, same pixels on every page at
  72 dpi — and equal content set digests. The raw bytes differ in **6 bytes
  per part, every one inside the `/CreationDate` and `/ModDate` values** of the
  information dictionary (classification `info-dates-only` for all 50 pairs).
  Nothing is masked; the raw differences are recorded, never compared.
- The comparison's controls, all as expected: RC-1 a byte copy → identical;
  RC-2 the second part → structure differs; RC-3 one digit of the creation
  date changed → content-identical, dates only; RC-4 one letter of the title
  changed → content-identical, other bytes.

Unlike PDFsharp's output in the earlier experiment, Chromium's parts carry no
random field beyond the two print dates.

### Library behaviour checked before measuring

On a 2-part, 10-page probe set:

- **Text extraction:** `pdftotext -bbox` returns, on V1 and V2 outputs, the
  same content words as the source page on every page, exactly one "Page k of
  10" (the part's own number), and on V2 exactly `Page i of 20` inside the
  numbering area (stamp words at x 272.31–322.60, y 20.92–30.23 pt). The parts
  embed three Liberation Sans subsets (regular, bold, italic) as Type0 fonts
  with Identity-H encoding, each with a `/ToUnicode` map.
- **What stamping adds:** the stamped page's content goes from 1 to 2 content
  streams.
- **Closing the sources:** each imported document can be closed right after
  its pages are added and becomes collectable while the output is open.
- **Structure:** the untouched part, V1 and V4 `pass-clean`; V2 `pass-exempt`
  (81 lines), V5 `pass-exempt` (82 lines).

- **Structural check (H1).** The zlib warning came from PDFsharp's streams:

  the producer and the assembly library, not from the merge itself.

  and the staged variant (V3) are within about ±7 %; qpdf (V4) uses 18–26 %

## Conclusion

No decision is taken here; these are the figures the assembly choice can use
on Chromium-printed parts.

- **H1 holds for the parts and for merge-only outputs, not for stamped
  ones.** Every Chromium part and every V1 and V4 output passes the
  structural check clean; every output PDFsharp stamps carries the zlib
  warning again (4 lines per stamped page). A strict validator gate on stamped
  output would still need the exact-message narrowing.
- **H2 holds as to order of magnitude.** Every variant stays below 0.35 GiB of
  peak RSS and 7.3 s at 10 000 pages under the 8 GiB / 4 CPU limit, with no
  failed run. qpdf is lighter than before; single-pass merge + stamp in
  PDFsharp is the heaviest variant here (335 MiB at 10 000 pages, ×1.52 the
  earlier figure). **None of the five variants is bounded** by the convention.
- **H3 holds** against the matching reference: 0 changed pixels outside the
  numbering area on every checked page, with page order and both numbers
  correct on every checked page. Against the unedited source page it does not
  hold for any output PDFsharp wrote, because of the page-box rewrite on
  import — a rasterisation effect of the height value, not a content change.
- **H4 holds.** The earlier bottom band holds the page-subtotal footer on
  every page; a stamp there would overlap it.
- The input reproduces at content level and differs in raw bytes only by the
  print dates.

## Limitations

- One producer configuration (Paged.js, table markup, rows kept whole,
  page-subtotal footer on); native Chromium layout is not measured.
- **Ordinary rows only** — no page-spanning row; the real row mix is not
  reproduced (see *Input rows*).
- Parts are independent layouts: each part's brought-forward amount starts at
  0.00.
- The stamp uses DejaVu Sans on Liberation Sans content.
- One machine, one day, three repetitions per combination.
- The structural check tolerates one exact zlib warning text and is not a
  proof of stream integrity.
- The pixel check at 5 000 and 10 000 pages covers 30 sampled pages; words and
  page boxes are checked on every page.
- The managed heap is sampled every 20 ms, so a shorter peak can be missed;
  the process peak RSS from GNU `time` is exact.
